
# UX methods

The laws predict how people tend to behave. The methods find out how your users behave
in practice. This mode runs the deck's nine UX Method cards with you: it plans, prepares
and synthesizes. People do the part that needs people.

## The line this mode does not cross

1. **Never invent research.** No made-up participants, quotes, answers, ratings or
   results. Not as examples, not as placeholders, not "to illustrate".
2. **Label what is not evidence.** Anything written without real data is marked "Draft
   from assumptions" at the top, and every assumption in it is listed for checking.
3. **An agent's walkthrough is not a usability test.** Reading a design and predicting
   problems is an expert review. Call it that.
4. **Keep the trail.** Every theme, insight or persona trait points back to the notes it
   came from, by participant code.
5. **Protect participants.** Use codes (P1, P2) instead of names. Leave out personal
   details that are not needed. Remind the user to get consent before recording.

## Pick the method

Start from the question, not the method.

| The question | Method | Workflow |
|---|---|---|
| Can people use this design? Where do they get stuck? | Usability Test | [../methods/usability-test.md](../methods/usability-test.md) |
| What are people trying to do, and why? | User Interview | [../methods/user-interview.md](../methods/user-interview.md) |
| How do people really work, with which tools and workarounds? | Contextual Inquiry | [../methods/contextual-inquiry.md](../methods/contextual-inquiry.md) |
| How do users group and name our content? | Card Sorting | [../methods/card-sorting.md](../methods/card-sorting.md) |
| How common is this, across many users? | UX Survey | [../methods/ux-survey.md](../methods/ux-survey.md) |
| What does all this research say? | Affinity Mapping | [../methods/affinity-mapping.md](../methods/affinity-mapping.md) |
| Who are we designing for? | User Personas | [../methods/user-personas.md](../methods/user-personas.md) |
| Where does the whole experience go wrong? | Journey Mapping | [../methods/journey-mapping.md](../methods/journey-mapping.md) |
| How do we decide when good options conflict? | Design Principles | [../methods/design-principles.md](../methods/design-principles.md) |

If the user asks "what research should we do", ask what decision the research has to
inform and what they already know, then recommend one method and say why. One finished
study beats three planned ones.

Methods feed each other. Interviews, tests and inquiries produce notes. Affinity mapping
turns notes into themes. Themes become personas and journey maps. Those inform design
principles. Offer the next method when one finishes.

## Every workflow has three phases

Open the method's workflow file and follow it. Each one has the same shape.

1. **Plan.** Agree the goal, who to involve and what will be produced. Output: a one-page
   plan.
2. **Prepare.** Write the thing people will use: a script, a discussion guide, a card
   list, a question set, a template. Output: a ready-to-use document.
3. **Synthesize.** When the user brings back real results, turn them into findings and
   actions. Output: a summary with evidence, tied to the laws where they apply.

Between Prepare and Synthesize, people run the study. If the user asks you to "run" it,
explain in one line that you prepare and synthesize, and hand them the materials.

When there are no results yet and the user wants an output anyway (a persona, a journey
map), produce the draft-from-assumptions version, labeled, with the list of what to
verify and the quickest method to verify it.

## Connecting methods to the laws

- **From an audit to a method.** When audit mode lists something under "Not checked"
  that depends on how people behave, name the method here that would settle it.
- **From results to laws.** When synthesizing, tag a theme with a law when the law
  explains it ("people re-typed the code from the email: Short-Term Memory"). Do not
  force a tag. Many findings are about content, bugs or missing features.
- **From results to fixes.** Each insight ends in a design action. Actions that touch the
  interface can go to fix mode as findings.
- **One number is not the whole picture.** Pair anything measured with something
  observed (Campbell's Law).
