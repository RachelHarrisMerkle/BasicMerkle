# Usability Test

UX Method card.

Related cards in the deck: [Jakob's Law](jakobs-law.md), [Affinity Mapping](affinity-mapping.md).

## In one line

Watching representative users attempt realistic tasks with a design, to find problems and learn how people behave.

## The steps

- Recruit real users: people who represent who the product is for. Do not rely only on colleagues, unless the product is for colleagues.
- Simulate real tasks: ask participants to do realistic things with the design. Tasks can be specific or open, depending on the goal.
- Observe and listen: stay neutral, avoid leading questions, make clear that the design is being tested and not the person, and resist helping when they get stuck.
- Measure: record how fast and how easily people complete each task, as well as what they say. Performance and opinion often differ.

## Reach for it when

- An audit found issues that depend on how people behave, and inspection cannot settle them.
- A polished design may be hiding problems (Aesthetic-Usability Effect).
- Before and after any significant change. Six of the deck's seven interaction principle cards name this method as a related card.

## Questions it answers

- Can people complete the main tasks, and where do they fail?
- Do people understand the labels, icons and structure?
- Did the fix work?

## What an agent can and cannot do

An agent can write the plan, tasks, script, consent wording and observation sheet, and can synthesize real session notes. It cannot be the participant. An agent "walking through" a design is an expert review, which finds different problems from a test with users, and must not be reported as a usability test.
