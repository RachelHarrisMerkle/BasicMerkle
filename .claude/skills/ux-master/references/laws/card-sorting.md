# Card Sorting

UX Method card.

Related cards in the deck: [Hick's Law](hicks-law.md), [Contextual Inquiry](contextual-inquiry.md).

## In one line

A research method where participants sort topics into groups and name the groups, so the structure of a product matches how its users think.

## The steps

- Gather: list the items to be organized, such as navigation destinations or product types, one per card.
- Sort: ask each participant to put the cards into groups that make sense to them, thinking aloud as they go.
- Label: ask them to name each group. The names show the words users would look for.
- Dig deeper: ask why they grouped things as they did, what was hard to place and what they left out.

## Reach for it when

- Navigation has many items and needs grouping (Hick's Law, Chunking).
- People cannot find things, or labels come from inside the company (Mental Model, Conceptual Model).
- Before building or restructuring menus, categories, settings sections or a help center.

## Questions it answers

- Which items do users expect to find together?
- What do users call each group?
- Which items have no obvious home?

## What an agent can and cannot do

An agent can prepare the card list, the session script and the recording sheet, and can analyze real results. It cannot stand in for participants. A sort produced by the agent alone is a hypothesis about structure, and must be labeled as one.
