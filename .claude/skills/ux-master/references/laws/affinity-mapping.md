# Affinity Mapping

UX Method card.

Related cards in the deck: [User Interview](user-interview.md).

## In one line

Sorting qualitative notes into groups on an affinity diagram to find themes and insights.

## The steps

- Record: put each observation, quote or fact from the research on its own note.
- Identify: look for notes that relate and cluster them. Move quickly; groups can change.
- Label: name each group by its theme, written as a finding, such as "people often did X when trying to do Y".
- Insights: say what the themes mean together, support each with evidence from the notes, and attach a design action to each.

## Reach for it when

- After interviews, usability tests, surveys with open answers, or contextual inquiry.
- When several people need to agree on what the research said.
- To turn audit findings from several screens into themes.

## Questions it answers

- What patterns run through the research?
- Which themes have the strongest evidence?
- What should we do about each?

## What an agent can and cannot do

This is the method an agent is best placed to help with: it can cluster real notes quickly and show its reasoning. It must keep every note traceable to its source, must not add notes of its own, and should present clusters as a draft for people to challenge.

## Run it

To plan this method, write its script or template and synthesize the results, use the workflow in `../methods/affinity-mapping.md`.

Background: Based on the KJ method of Jiro Kawakita (1960s).
