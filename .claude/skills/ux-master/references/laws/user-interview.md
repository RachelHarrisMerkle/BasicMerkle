# User Interview

UX Method card.

Related cards in the deck: [Jakob's Law](jakobs-law.md), [Affinity Mapping](affinity-mapping.md).

## In one line

A one-to-one conversation in which a researcher asks a user about a topic to learn about their behavior and attitudes.

## The steps

- Define the goal: decide exactly what you want to learn, tied to one aspect of behavior or attitude.
- Prepare a discussion guide: write questions around the goal, and be ready to follow up on what you hear. Themes can work better than a rigid list.
- Build rapport: put the person at ease before the real questions. People share more when they trust the interviewer.
- Avoid leading questions: ask open questions that start with what, how or when, or "tell me about". Avoid yes or no questions and vague ones.

## Reach for it when

- You do not know why people behave as the data shows.
- You need to learn the user's mental model before designing structure or naming (Mental Model, Conceptual Model).
- Early discovery, and after launch to understand what the numbers mean (Campbell's Law).

## Questions it answers

- What are people trying to get done, and what gets in the way?
- What words do they use?
- What do they expect a product like this to do?

## What an agent can and cannot do

An agent can draft the goal, the guide and the follow-up prompts, check questions for bias and synthesize real transcripts. It must never invent interview answers or quotes. Simulated interviews are not research.
