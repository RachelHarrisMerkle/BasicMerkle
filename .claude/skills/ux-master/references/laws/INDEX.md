# Laws of UX card index

Each card is a file in this folder. Open a card's file only when that card is in play. Name at most four or five laws for one problem. Every card file has the same sections: In one line, What it asks of an interface, When it applies, Detect a violation, Example, When laws conflict, Origin.

Numbers come from the source named beside them. Where no source is named, the number is a working heuristic. Say which is which when you quote one.

## Find a card by situation

| Situation | Cards to open |
|---|---|
| Too many options, people cannot decide | Hick's Law, Analysis Paralysis, Von Restorff Effect |
| The screen feels overwhelming | Cognitive Load, Chunking, Occam's Razor, Law of Proximity |
| People must remember something | Short-Term Memory, Miller's Law, Chunking |
| Buttons or links are hard to hit | Fitts' Law, Accot-Zhai Steering Law |
| People do not notice the important thing | Selective Attention, Von Restorff Effect, Serial Position Effect |
| People do not know what is clickable or what an icon means | Affordances, Signifiers, Discoverability |
| Nothing seems to happen after a click | Feedback, Doherty Threshold |
| It works differently from what people expect | Jakob's Law, Mental Model, Conceptual Model, Cognitive Dissonance |
| Layout does not show what belongs together | Law of Proximity, Law of Common Region, Law of Similarity, Law of Uniform Connectedness, Law of Continuity |
| A form rejects reasonable input | Postel's Law, Constraints, Tesler's Law |
| A form or task takes too long | Principle of Least Effort, Parkinson's Law, Tesler's Law |
| People drop out of a multi-step flow | Goal-Gradient Effect, Zeigarnik Effect, Flow |
| The ending or the error moments are poor | Peak-End Rule, Feedback |
| Controls sit far from what they affect | Mapping, Simon Effect |
| A control's look contradicts what it does | Stroop Effect, Affordances |
| Nobody reads the onboarding | Paradox of the Active User, Discoverability |
| It looks good but tests badly, or the reverse | Aesthetic-Usability Effect |
| A design is steering people, or chasing a metric | Cognitive Bias, Campbell's Law |
| Deciding what to fix or build first | Pareto Principle |
| We do not know what users think or do | The nine UX Method cards, through `../modes/research.md` |
