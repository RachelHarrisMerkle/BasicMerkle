# UX Survey

UX Method card.

Related cards in the deck: [Usability Test](usability-test.md), [Contextual Inquiry](contextual-inquiry.md).

## In one line

A set of questions sent to many users to collect data about their experience with a product.

## The steps

- Set expectations: tell participants what the survey is about, how long it takes and how to get in touch.
- Questions: keep them short and simple. Mix closed questions with fixed answers and open questions for free response. Ask one thing per question.
- Bias: avoid wording that leads or primes. Ask people to describe their experience with a feature, not to list the problems they had with it.
- Ease in: start with broad, easy questions and move to the more demanding ones later.

## Reach for it when

- You need to know how common a problem or attitude is.
- You want a baseline measure before a change and a comparison after.
- You want to recruit participants for interviews or tests.

## Questions it answers

- How many people experience this, and how strongly?
- How do different user groups compare?

## What an agent can and cannot do

An agent can draft and bias-check the questions, order them, and analyze real responses. It cannot produce responses. Surveys measure what people say, not what they do, so pair survey results with observation (Campbell's Law).
