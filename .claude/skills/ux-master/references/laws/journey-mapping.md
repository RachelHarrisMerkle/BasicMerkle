# Journey Mapping

UX Method card.

Related cards in the deck: [Peak-End Rule](peak-end-rule.md), [Affinity Mapping](affinity-mapping.md).

## In one line

A picture of the steps a person goes through to reach a goal, with what they do, think and feel at each one.

## The steps

- Lens: name the person, the scenario and what they expect. For example, someone ordering a ride who expects it at their exact location within ten minutes.
- Experience: lay out their actions across a timeline in phases, and plot how they feel at each step, from delight to frustration.
- Insights: for each low point, name the opportunity to improve it, who inside the organization owns it, and the metric that would show it worked.

## Reach for it when

- A flow spans several screens, channels or days, and no one sees it end to end.
- You need to find the emotional peaks and the ending (Peak-End Rule).
- Before prioritizing fixes across a whole product.

## Questions it answers

- Where are the worst moments, and who owns fixing them?
- What does the person expect at each step, and where is that expectation broken?
- How will we know an improvement worked?

## What an agent can and cannot do

An agent can build the map structure, walk a flow from code or screens to list the steps, and mark likely pain points as assumptions. The feelings on a real journey map come from research with users. Until that exists, the emotional line is a guess and must be marked as one.
