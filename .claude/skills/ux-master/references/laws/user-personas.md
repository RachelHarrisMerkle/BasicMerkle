# User Personas

UX Method card.

Related cards in the deck: [Jakob's Law](jakobs-law.md), [Contextual Inquiry](contextual-inquiry.md).

## In one line

A fictional but research-based profile whose characteristics and goals stand for a larger group of real users.

## The steps

- Info: give the persona a name, a photo or sketch, a short tagline and basic details such as age and occupation, reflecting what the group has in common, so the team can remember them.
- Details: write a short bio and list behaviors, goals, motivations, frustrations and the tasks they would do with the product.
- Insights: add attitudes and context, ideally with direct quotes from research.

## Reach for it when

- Decisions are being made for "the user" with no agreement on who that is.
- You need to judge which conventions your users already know (Jakob's Law, Mental Model).
- Before prioritizing features or writing usability test tasks.

## Questions it answers

- Who are the main groups of users, and how do their goals differ?
- What does each group already know and expect?

## What an agent can and cannot do

An agent can structure a persona and fill it from real research notes, interview transcripts or analytics. A persona written without research is a proto-persona: a set of assumptions to be checked. The agent must label it that way and list what needs validating.
